# Guru Check

A beginner-friendly checklist for judging a course, a Skool group, or a paid community before you pay. Follow the money, trace PR claims back to their first source, and write down what you could not verify.

The easy question is "is this person a scammer?" Funnels are built to survive that question. The video is real. The income screenshot might be real. Someone, somewhere, once made money the way the page describes. The money you send can still be gone, because you inspected the one kind of proof the page was designed to show you.

Guru Check asks a narrower question: **does the mechanism that made this person money still work when you run it yourself, and will they still be there when you need them?**

It also does the opposite job. A lot of quiet practitioners fail every red-flag list because those lists punish silence. This one has a way to notice them.

## The framework

Guru Check is not a score. Two offers can earn the same number of red flags for opposite reasons, and one score would hide that. It is a set of six lenses, used in order:

1. **Follow the money, not the trophy.** What share of their income comes from doing the work, and what share comes from teaching it? A practitioner who teaches on the side is a different product from a teacher whose only checkable income is tuition.
2. **Judge by the students, not the founder.** Find one named student, starting from a place like yours, with a result you can see outside the founder's own channels. Praise for the vibe is not a result.
3. **Check whether the room is still alive.** The founder goes quiet before the community does. Look at when they last replied to someone, not when they last posted. Posts can be scheduled. Replies are harder to fake for long.
4. **Trace PR to its first source.** Podcasts, press logos, and review sites often repeat the founder. Use the evidence ladder in `SKILL.md`: self-reported, founder-supplied, repeated, independently supported. Only the last one counts as proof.
5. **Audit the guarantee against the terms.** Quote the promise and the refund policy side by side, with the date you read each. If they do not match, ask which one controls the checkout. A mismatch is a question first, not a verdict.
6. **Run the hidden-gem countercheck.** Before you reject a quiet teacher, look for peers naming them without being paid to, a reputation older than the hot niche, and proof they still do the work. Silence alone proves nothing, in either direction.

At the end, the **scam label gate** decides what words you may use in public. Scammer and fraud need a regulator or court record, a payment processor finding, or several independent customers with receipts. Below that line, write what you actually have: unverified claim, inconsistent figures, marketing overreach, not enough evidence.

## Why this matters

A funnel only has to pass the checks most buyers already run: member count, testimonials on the sales page, and a search for the name plus the word scam. All three can be arranged. Member counts can be pumped in a 30-day sprint. Testimonials are selected by the seller. Search results are shaped by the seller's own SEO and affiliates.

PR makes the problem harder. Authority can be built by repetition rather than by proof, and a beginner has no way to tell the difference from the outside. This framework gives a beginner the same move a careful buyer makes: stop asking whether the person looks credible, and ask where each claim came from and who else, who owes them nothing, has confirmed it.

## How to use it, step by step

You need one link and about 20 minutes. A sales page, a Skool about page, or a founder profile is enough to start.

**Minutes 0 to 3: write the claim ledger.** Copy the biggest promise exactly as written. The income number, the timeframe, the guarantee. Note the date and the page. Do not paraphrase yet. Exact words are what you will test.

**Minutes 3 to 8: trace each number to its first source.** Search the exact number in quotes. Open the oldest result you can find. If a podcast, a profile, and a review site all point back to the founder saying it, label it self-reported and move on. That label is a result, not a failure.

**Minutes 8 to 12: search outside their channels.** Search the name and exclude their own site and social accounts. You are looking for a peer naming them without being asked, a named student with a result, or a complaint with a receipt. One independent source outweighs ten pages that repeat the founder.

**Minutes 12 to 16: open the terms and look at the room.** Read the refund section, not the guarantee in the headline. On Skool, check the future event calendar, when the classroom was last updated, and whether members start threads that other members answer. If the group is private, write that down as something you could not verify.

**Minutes 16 to 20: write the verdict in the fixed format.** Label, verdict (BUY, WAIT, or SKIP), why, money at risk, next action. Then write the section most people skip: what you could not verify. If you cannot name one thing in that section, you have not finished.

Short on time? Do steps 1, 2, and 5. An exact claim, its first source, and an honest list of gaps will protect you better than an hour of reading testimonials.

Using an AI agent? Copy this folder into your agent skills directory, or paste `SKILL.md` into the chat with the link and ask it to run the check before you pay.

## A honest notice about evidence

Sometimes this check ends with "not enough evidence," and that is the correct answer. Founders can hide income behind private dashboards. Communities sit behind a login. Students rarely publish results with their real names. When the evidence is not there, Guru Check will say WAIT instead of inventing confidence. A skill that always gives a clean verdict is a skill that is guessing.

That limit is why feedback matters here. This framework gets better from real cases, not from more theory.

## Help make it better

- Found a claim this check would mislabel? Open an issue with the exact quote, the link, and the date you read it.
- Found a counterexample, a quiet teacher it would wrongly reject, or a polished seller it would wrongly pass? That is the most valuable kind of report.
- Found a stronger source for a number, or a weaker one that collapses on opening? Send the first origin, not the fourth repeat.
- Disagree with a label or a threshold? Say which line, and what evidence would change it.

Corrections are welcome in the open. If a line in this repo is wrong, the fix should be visible to the next beginner who reads it.

## What it will not do

- It will not call a named person a fraud as a fact. It records signals, and it says when a point is still unchecked.
- It will not join, email, or buy for you.
- It is not legal advice and it is not a financial audit.
- A name on a roast list is not a verdict. Run the checklist, then quote the line you can actually open.
